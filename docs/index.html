What it does

One window, three steps.

No scripts, no file conversions, no command line. The parts that usually take an afternoon — cleaning the protein, building the box, generating 3D conformers, writing PDBQT files — happen while you watch.

STEP 01

The protein

Type a PDB code or upload your own .pdb, .cif or .mol2. AKADock finds the bound ligands and builds the docking box around the one you pick. No ligand? Name the pocket residues instead.

STEP 02

The compounds

Draw them in the built-in editor, paste SMILES, upload an SDF from PubChem, or download the measured actives for your target straight from ChEMBL. Up to 500 in one run.

STEP 03

The answer

A ranked table with affinity, ligand efficiency and the residues each compound touches; every pose in 3D; a report, a spreadsheet and all the files for PyMOL or Chimera.

The part other tools skip

It proves the setup before it ranks anything.

Before your compounds are docked, AKADock removes the ligand that came with the crystal structure and docks it back. If the top pose lands on the crystal pose, the pocket, the box and the preparation are all sound — and the ranking means something.

If it does not, you are told in the same window, in the same run. A docking result you cannot check is just a number.

New in 1.1

Ask Claude to dock it.

AKADock ships an MCP server, so Claude Desktop and Claude Code can drive it: load a protein, dock a list of compounds, read the ranked results back — all on your own machine, with nothing uploaded.

One command registers it: python mcp\install_mcp.py. The engine starts itself, and a run you ask for in words appears under Previous runs like any other.

THE VERDICT TRAVELS WITH THE NUMBERS Every result handed to Claude carries the redocking verdict and a trust flag, and the tool tells the model in writing not to rank compounds from a run that failed its self-check.

Same engine, same files, same self-check — only the way you ask for it changes. A docking score from Claude is the same prediction as a docking score from the window.

Before you cite it

What docking cannot tell you.

A score is a prediction, not a measurement. It is not evidence that a compound binds or works. Results must be confirmed in the laboratory.

Differences under about 1 kcal/mol are noise. Published numbers for the same complex often differ by that much between laboratories.

Metal sites are docked without the metal. AutoDock Vina has no attractive term for metal coordination, so a pose in a metal pocket can be wrong with a score that looks fine. AKADock warns you when a metal falls inside the box.

Covalent binding is not docked. Vina reads a covalent bond as a clash, so AKADock measures how close the warhead gets to your residue instead of pretending to form the bond.

The protein is rigid unless you name flexible side chains, and even then the backbone never moves.

Protonation and tautomers are assigned automatically and can be wrong for unusual chemistry.
